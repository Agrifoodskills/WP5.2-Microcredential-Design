  Narration (0:00-0:45):
  A module can contain all the required information and still provide a weak learning experience. Content tells us what is available to learners. Learning design asks what learners will actually do with it.

  The central question for this session is simple: what must learners do in order to build and demonstrate the intended capability?

  type: ul
  items:
    - "**ABC:** storyboard the sequence and balance of learner activity"
    - "**ADDIE:** manage the design, development, delivery and improvement process"
    - "**UDL:** build accessibility, flexibility and options into learning from the outset"
notes: |
  Narration (0:45-2:00):
  These three frameworks operate at different levels.

  ABC helps a design team describe the learning journey as a sequence of activities. ADDIE provides a systematic process for moving from analysis through design and development to implementation and evaluation. Universal Design for Learning asks whether the resulting experience provides flexible ways to access content, participate and demonstrate learning.

  They are most useful when combined because they answer different design questions.

  Source: Section 4.2.

--- slide
id: s03-03
layout: Full
title: ABC storyboards the learning journey
full:
  type: ul
  items:
    - Sketch the main learning sequence before producing the materials
    - Describe each segment as something the learner does
    - Use a shared set of activity types
    - Review the balance and flow of the complete sequence
    - Prototype quickly, then refine for relevance and coherence
notes: |
  Narration (2:00-3:15):
  ABC is a rapid, collaborative learning-design approach. The design team creates a storyboard before writing the full script or producing resources.

  Each part of the module is described as a learner activity. This makes it possible to see whether a sequence relies too heavily on receiving information, whether learners have opportunities to test ideas, and whether the journey leads towards meaningful practice or production.

  The storyboard is deliberately quick to produce. Its purpose is to make the learning journey visible early enough to change it.

  Source: Section 4.2.1.

id: s03-04
layout: Comparison
title: Six types of learning activity
left_head: Acquisition · Investigation · Discussion
left:
  type: ul
  items:
    - "**Acquisition:** read, listen or watch"
    - "**Investigation:** explore, compare or research"
    - "**Discussion:** articulate and debate ideas"
right_head: Collaboration · Practice · Production
right:
  type: ul
  items:
    - "**Collaboration:** work with others on a shared task"
    - "**Practice:** apply a skill in a controlled setting"
    - "**Production:** create a report, plan, demonstration or other output"
notes: |
  Narration (3:15-5:00):
  ABC uses six activity types.

  Acquisition is learning through reading, listening or watching. Investigation asks learners to explore evidence or resources. Discussion requires them to articulate and test ideas with others. Collaboration involves shared responsibility for a task or output. Practice allows skills to be applied with feedback. Production requires learners to create something that represents their developing capability.

  Acquisition is necessary, but it is not the whole learning journey. The activity mix should reflect what learners will eventually need to do, not an assumption that all six types must receive equal time.

  Source: Section 4.2.1.

left_head: Content-led sequence
left:
  type: ol
  items:
    - Present the information
    - Ask learners to recall it
    - Move to the next topic
right_head: Activity-led sequence
right:
  type: ol
  items:
    - Acquire essential information
    - Investigate a relevant case or source
    - Discuss alternative interpretations
    - Practise the required decision or skill
    - Produce an output that can be reviewed
notes: |
  Narration (5:00-6:15):
  The same subject matter can support very different learning experiences.

  A content-led sequence is organised around what the educator intends to present. An activity-led sequence is organised around how learners will engage with information, test their reasoning, practise and produce evidence of learning.

  This does not mean removing explanation or reading. Acquisition provides essential material. The change is that acquisition becomes one stage in a journey towards application rather than the endpoint of the design.

  Source: Sections 4.2.1 and 5.2.1.

--- slide
id: s03-06
layout: Full
title: ADDIE provides the development cycle
full:
  type: ol
  items:
    - "**Analyse:** learners, needs, context, objectives and resources"
    - "**Design:** content, activities, assessment and media"
    - "**Develop:** create or assemble the learning materials"
    - "**Implement:** deliver the learning experience"
    - "**Evaluate:** assess effectiveness and revise"
notes: |
  Narration (6:15-7:45):
  ADDIE provides a systematic cycle for instructional design.

  Analysis establishes who the learners are, what they already know, what problems they need to solve and what resources are available. Design turns those findings into a coherent plan. Development creates or assembles the materials. Implementation puts the design into use. Evaluation tests whether the learning achieved its purpose and informs revision.

  The cycle prevents a common error: beginning with material production before the learner, purpose and evidence of success have been defined.

  Source: Section 4.2.2.

  Narration (7:45-8:30):
  Accessibility is more effective when it is designed into the learning experience rather than added later as an individual adjustment.

  Universal Design for Learning anticipates variation between learners and reduces avoidable barriers while preserving the intended learning challenge.

--- slide
id: s03-08
layout: Full
title: The three UDL principles
full:
  type: ul
  items:
    - text: "**Multiple means of representation — the what of learning**"
      items:
        - Provide information in different forms, such as text, audio, video or diagrams
    - text: "**Multiple means of action and expression — the how of learning**"
      items:
        - Offer different ways to practise and demonstrate understanding
    - text: "**Multiple means of engagement — the why of learning**"
      items:
        - Provide relevant choices, collaborative opportunities and timely feedback
notes: |
  Narration (8:30-10:15):
  Multiple means of representation provide more than one way to perceive and understand information. Multiple means of action and expression provide different ways to interact with learning and demonstrate understanding. Multiple means of engagement provide options that support interest, motivation and self-regulation.

  Choice is not the removal of standards. Learners can sometimes use different routes or forms of expression while being judged against the same intended capability and performance criteria.

  Source: Section 4.2.3.

id: s03-09
layout: Comparison
title: Use the frameworks together
left_head: Design question
left:
  type: ul
  items:
    - What must learners do?
    - How will the learning be developed and improved?
    - How will barriers, options and learner differences be addressed?
right_head: Framework response
right:
  type: ul
  items:
    - "**ABC:** map and balance the learner activities"
    - "**ADDIE:** manage the complete development cycle"
    - "**UDL:** build flexible access, participation and expression into the design"
notes: |
  Narration (10:15-12:15):
  Use ABC when the immediate question is what learners will do and how those activities form a coherent journey.

  Use ADDIE to manage the wider process: analysing the need, designing the experience, developing resources, implementing the programme and evaluating its effectiveness.

  Use UDL throughout both processes to identify barriers and build meaningful options into representation, action, expression and engagement.

  The frameworks overlap, but they are not interchangeable. ABC describes the activity pattern. ADDIE describes the development cycle. UDL supplies an accessibility and inclusion lens across the design.

  Source: Section 4.2.

notes: |
  Narration (12:15-15:00):
  Select one short segment from a module you already teach. Begin by replacing the content heading with a learner action. Instead of “biosecurity principles,” for example, specify what learners will investigate, decide, practise or produce using those principles.

  Identify the ABC activity types currently present. Add only those needed to support the intended capability. Then review the segment through the UDL principles: can learners access the essential information, participate meaningfully and demonstrate the same capability through an appropriate route?

  Finally, define what you will evaluate after implementation. The result should be a small, testable redesign that can be refined—not a complete module rewrite.

  Pause the presentation while you record the four decisions.

  Source: Sections 4.2 and 5.2.2.
